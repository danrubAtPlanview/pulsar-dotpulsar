# 0016 - `ConsumerState.Active` is raised before the channel is usable

Status: **fixed in WP10** (pre-existing on master, medium severity: user-visible latency and flaky tests).

`ChannelManager` raises `Connected()` at the subscribe response (`ChannelManager.cs:90`), before `SubConsumer` assigns `_channel` (`SubConsumer.cs:312`). A `Receive` right after `StateChangedTo(ConsumerState.Active)` can therefore hit `ChannelNotReadyException` and waits the default 3 s retry interval (measured 3004-3024 ms, against 5-24 ms when the channel is already set, in about a third of the runs of the WP10 transaction tests). Readers and failover consumers have the same window. Tests with 2 s or 5 s receive timeouts right after `Active` are exposed; the WP10 tests that were found to be exposed use longer first-receive waits (`testReadCommittedWithCompaction`: 10 s) or create the transaction after the receive (`ackCommitTest`).
Suggested fix after parity: assign `_channel` before the state change is published (or raise `Connected()` after the assignment), plus a unit test that a `Receive` started inside the state-change callback does not wait the retry interval.

## More tests found exposed in the final verification (WP10)

Under the load of a full run of all the integration collections two more tests failed, because they waited only 3 s and 2 s for the first message of a new consumer right after `Active`: `TransactionEndToEndTestsBase.Receive_GivenMessagesInPendingAckStateAndSmallPrefetch_ShouldNotGetConsumerStuck` (3 s) and `BatchMessageWithBatchIndexLevelTests.Receive_GivenBatchThatIsHalfAcknowledgedAndRedelivered_ShouldNotLoseOrCreatePermits` (the drain loop stopped at the first 2 s without a message and the list was empty). Both now wait up to 10 s for the first message, and then use the old limit. The consumer also sends its first flow with the first `Receive` (`ConsumerChannel._sendWhenZero` starts at 0), so a consumer that is `Active` has no permits at the broker until someone receives; see followup 0015, where a test has to wait for the permits.

## Fix (WP10)

The suggested fix (assign `_channel` before the state is published) was not used, because the channel object that the connection raises its events on (`Channel`) is not the one that the owner keeps (`ConsumerChannel`, made by the factory after the subscribe response). The base `Process` knows instead when the channel of the last connect is in place (`IsChannelEstablished`, set when `EstablishNewChannel` has returned, cleared when the channel is disconnected or closed by the server), and `ConsumerProcess` and `ReaderProcess` publish `Active`, `Inactive` and `Connected` only then. The events that the channel raised before are applied when the channel is established. `ProducerProcess` is not changed.

Tests: `ProcessReconnectTests` (the state is not published before the channel is established; a disconnect while the channel is established is handled; consumer and reader) and `ConsumerStateTests.Receive_GivenReceiveRightAfterTheConsumerIsActive_ShouldNotWaitForTheRetryInterval`, which uses the exception pipeline of the client with its retry interval of 3 seconds, a connection that holds the owner back for 200 ms after the subscribe response, and fails without the change (`TimeoutException`) and passes with it. The two integration tests that had a first-receive wait of 10 seconds because of this have their original limits again and passed 15 of 15 runs. CHANGELOG: Fixed.

Found by the full integration run, not by the unit tests: for a Failover subscription the broker sends the change of the active consumer (`CommandActiveConsumerChange`) before it answers the subscribe command. The process therefore sees `ChannelActivated` first, and the `ChannelConnected` that follows used to overwrite `ChannelState`; the already published `Active` simply stayed. With the state waiting for the channel, the information would have been lost and a failover consumer would never have become active (5 integration tests had failed on their limit of 3 to 5 minutes when I stopped that run). `Process.Handle` therefore does not let `ChannelConnected` replace `Active` or `Inactive` (a disconnect resets the state when a channel of an earlier connection was lost), with two unit tests (active and inactive). All the integration tests with Failover in their name passed afterwards (10 passed, 1 skipped by design).
